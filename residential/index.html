No Surprises

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Pleasanton Water Heater offers honest, upfront pricing for every home, covering everything from a burned-out heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a thorough assessment of your unit, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and valve and tank condition, followed by a plain-English recommendation with the price spelled out before any work starts.

The problem with water heater failures is that most of them start inside the tank where you can't see them. Hard-water minerals build a crust on the tank floor, the anode rod wears down a little at a time, and when it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. When hot water comes out rusty, the tank rumbles, or a small drip appears at the base, get it looked at while it's still a simple repair.

Not every call is an emergency. Many homeowners call us to evaluate an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with regular descaling), a replacement on your own timeline beats scrambling for a new heater with water on the floor. We'll give you an honest read on how much life your equipment has left.

Maintenance Plans

Maintenance Plans for Pleasanton Homes

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing year-round. Regular service heads off failures before they start, flushes out mineral sediment, and catches small faults while they're still cheap to fix. At Pleasanton Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your actual heater, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a burner and flue check on gas units, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a temperature check at the tap.

Most homes do best with a yearly flush, and houses on hard water may need two. We adjust the schedule to the age of your equipment, how hard your household runs it, and the mineral content of your water, which determines how fast sediment builds up and how long an anode rod lasts.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a consistent maintenance schedule helps. A clean tank heats water faster with less noise, a healthy anode rod protects the steel from corrosion, and a working relief valve protects the whole house. Most families find that annual service costs far less than replacing a water heater even one year early.

Proper Sizing

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

An undersized water heater runs out halfway through the second shower, while an oversized one burns fuel keeping extra gallons hot around the clock. Pleasanton Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means looking at the first-hour rating to your busiest hour, rather than simply copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the gallons per minute against the fixtures you run at once and the temperature of your incoming water.

Picking a new water heater is about more than tank size. Our technician will compare tank models with tankless units, and gas with electric or heat pump, walking through operating costs, venting and gas line work, electrical service, and the installation itself. Tankless works well for many households, though it often requires a bigger gas line and new venting, while a well-sized tank can be the simpler, less expensive option. The decision is yours, based on your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

The majority of service calls we get come down to a handful of recurring problems. The water never gets hot. The pilot lights and then goes out a minute later. The shower goes cold after a few minutes, the tank rumbles and pops, or the tankless unit locks out with an error code. Add rusty water, sulfur smells, and dripping relief valves, and you have the full list. We fix every one of them, starting with the exact part that failed.

Not every water heater problem is equally urgent. A failed element just cuts your hot water supply. A relief valve spitting water, on the other hand, signals that pressure or temperature inside the tank is too high, and water pooling under the tank can quietly ruin subfloors and drywall before anyone notices. Smell gas? Leave the house and call your gas utility before anything else. Our technicians carry the parts to replace elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, install expansion tanks where pressure is the problem, and handle leak repair at the drain valve or fittings.
