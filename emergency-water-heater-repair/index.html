How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A heater breaker that will not stay on after being reset
  • Every tap running cold and the heater showing no signs of life
  • Hot water far hotter than normal, a sign of a runaway thermostat or gas control
  • Standing water around the water heater that returns after you wipe it up
  • Loud popping, banging, or rumbling from the tank that has gotten worse quickly
  • A rotten-egg smell near a gas water heater (leave first, and call the gas utility)
  • Water or steam coming from the temperature and pressure relief valve or its discharge pipe
  • Scorch marks, soot, or flames rolling out near the burner access on a gas unit

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A water heater emergency packs several hazards into one closet or garage corner. There is water on the floor near a 240-volt circuit on electric models, or near a gas valve, burner, and flue on gas models. There is a tank holding water at high temperature and household pressure. And there is a safety valve that, if it is discharging, is doing so for a reason. Homeowners sometimes try to stop the relief valve by capping it, tighten a leaking fitting until it cracks, or relight a pilot beside a gas smell, and each of those can turn a bad afternoon into a real disaster. The safest moves for a homeowner are the simple ones: leave for gas, then shut off water and energy, then call for help.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and a truck full of the likely parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution rather than guessing, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Equally important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a few hundred dollars versus a new heater. You get that answer before any work begins.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • Drain a few gallons or flush the tank annually to keep sediment from cooking the tank floor
  • A simple water sensor on the floor beside the tank can warn you hours earlier
  • Set the temperature around 120F, which protects against burns and slows tank wear
  • Ask about a drain pan and drain line, especially for heaters in attics, closets, or above finished rooms
  • Store chemicals and flammables far from the burner, and keep the area around the heater uncluttered
  • Find and label the cold-water shutoff at the heater and the main house valve now, before you ever need them
  • Test the T&P valve annually; a valve that sticks or weeps afterward should be replaced
