A cold shower from a gas water heater usually means one thing: the burner is not firing, and something told it not to. Gas heaters are built around safety. The control valve will not open unless a thermocouple or flame sensor proves there is a flame, and many models lock out if the flame arrestor screen at the bottom gets clogged or the heater senses spilled exhaust. So the real question is which safety did its job, and why. Across Pleasanton, CA, the answer is most often a worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot or burner, a lint-choked air intake, a failing gas valve, or a vent that is no longer drafting properly.

How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe
  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • Rust-colored hot water or rust streaks on the tank
  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • A pilot that lights, then goes out when you release the knob
  • Less hot water than the tank should deliver
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one
  • Loud rumbling from the bottom of the tank

Consider the risks first. Every gas water heater involves fuel piping, an open flame, and exhaust that contains carbon monoxide. Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Keep the thermostat around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
  • Vacuum lint and dust from the flame arrestor screen at the base a few times a year
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
