Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in San Jose

Many premature tank failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that point and is also showing discolored water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Hot Water Headaches San Jose Homeowners Face

Long before a tank floods a San Jose garage, it usually shows warning signs. Watch for a popping or rumbling sound while the tank heats, rust-colored hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, a damp spot under the tank, or showers that run cold sooner every month. These are early signs of sediment buildup, a spent anode rod, a failing element, or corrosion starting at the bottom seam, and when they're caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Left alone, they end in a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it needs a professional look soon. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
