Restaurant Services

Restaurant and Food Service Water Heaters

In a restaurant, the dish machine, the sinks, and the handwashing stations all pull from the same supply, and an undersized or failing heater shows up first as a dish machine that can't hit temperature and a backed-up dish room. A high-temp dish machine usually needs final rinse water near 180°F, which usually means a booster heater working alongside the main supply. We handle high-input tanks, tankless racks, booster heaters, and peak-demand sizing, with service scheduled around your hours.

Temperature is where many restaurant hot water problems start. If the main heater can't keep up, handwashing sinks go lukewarm, the three-compartment sink can't hold its wash temperature, and the dish machine struggles to sanitize at peak service. Our technicians verify temperatures from the tank all the way to the fixtures and log every visit in written reports your inspector and landlord can review.

Equipment replacements and maintenance schedules are planned with owners, managers, and kitchen leads, so everything fits the hours you're open. When a job can't happen during service, we install overnight or before opening, so the kitchen opens the next day with hot water ready.

Safe Installation

Safe Installations, Documented Service

Every commercial job we take on starts with safety. We inspect flue draft and vent connections, verify proper combustion air, test relief valves and their discharge lines, verify expansion tanks are charged correctly, and adjust mixing valves so water stored at higher temperatures leaves the tap at a safe temperature. We pull permits and schedule inspections where required. You receive a written report with priority levels and specific recommended actions.

Regular professional service protects your people and your property, and following through on our recommendations helps keep small issues from becoming emergencies. It also creates a record. We keep service logs, temperature records, and a documentation binder for every account, with more frequent visits for heavy-use equipment in kitchens, laundries, and mechanical rooms. When a landlord, insurer, or inspector asks for proof of due diligence, it's ready.
