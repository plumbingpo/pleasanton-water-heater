Here is what shortens most water heaters' lives: corrosion from the inside and mineral buildup on the bottom, both of which are largely preventable. As water heats, dissolved minerals come out of the water and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer insulates the water from the burner, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it buries the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, dissolves a little every month. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Pleasanton, CA, we see this pattern constantly, and most of it was preventable.

The work is hands-on and specific. We flush the tank until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we verify the expansion tank still holds its air charge, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

Tankless heaters skip the sediment bed but have a scale problem of their own. Minerals coat the heat exchanger over time, reducing efficiency and eventually setting off error codes. Our technicians connect a circulation pump to the isolation valves and run a descaling solution through the exchanger, clean the inlet screen, inspect the venting, and check the condensate drain on condensing models. When to Call for Water Heater Maintenance

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings
  • A tankless unit showing error codes or fluctuating temperatures
  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before
  • A water heater that has never been flushed or inspected
  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap
  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Ask about the anode rod at each visit; it is the tank's main defense against rust
  • Keep the thermostat near 120 degrees to limit scalding and slow sediment buildup
  • Keep at least a couple of feet clear around the heater for air and service access
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Look around the base of the heater periodically for moisture
  • Make an annual flush part of your home maintenance routine
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often in hard water
