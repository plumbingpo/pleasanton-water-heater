Keeping Your Fremont Water Heater Running Longer

Routine maintenance is the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows discolored water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone today so you can reach us fast as soon as something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
